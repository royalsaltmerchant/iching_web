I Ching Hexagram 60

䷻ Measured

Measured / Articulating / Limitation

Chinese節
Pinyinjié
Binary110010

Reading

Development comes from measuring by the times rather than the patterns of mind—well-measured discipline leads to progress.

Yin and yang lines are balanced, with yang energy holding the central position—the strong pursues, supported by the weak, so limits are pushed, but not exceeded. Well-measured disciplines lead to progress and attainment. Too severe, and eventually they will need to be relaxed, before the joy of practice becomes toil. Awareness of life’s purpose brings pleasure, and there are naturally no restrictions. By proceeding from this firm and well-placed footing, action moves freely. Measure by proper standards, and there will be no injury. Let your calmness and adaptability bloom, without losing mindfulness, and falling into arbitrary ways, or restricting patterns, and you will find balance. This is going along with the times, rather than one’s own mind.

Changing Lines

Line 1: Not leaving home without punishment.

Knowing right from wrong, then acting; this is movement without leaving home—understanding when the path is free, and when it is obstructed. Careful in the beginning, and there will be no error.

Line 2: Refusing to leave the home does not bode well.

Not moving beyond the boundaries, time and space to act is lost—refusal to act is without good. The weak leads the strong, so internal develops, without external being uplifted. Be effective by going outside yourself.

Line 3: You will lament not having followed suitable disciplines.

Craving for happiness, without working with discipline—pushing to attain externals, without establishing correct internal measures; these lead to self-inflicted wounds, with no one else to blame.

Line 4: Disciplines are quietly and naturally attended.

Deference to the proper source of authority leads to progress and success. Moving forward in peace, even those who are undisciplined can follow correct regulation, because they do not exert themselves by force.

Line 5: Disciplines are enacted with acceptance and contentment—there will be sweetness.

Inner mastery will lead to contentment, even in situations where suffering is great. Consistent measures and application, strong yet adaptable, will lead to good fortune, great progress, and positive affirmation. This is balance.

Line 6: The disciplines are too severe and difficult.

If the measure is over tuned, exhaustion and blame are the result. Fanatical adherence makes for strong but ineffective disciplines. Both the measures, and the negative outcomes, will eventually wear out. Do not be excessive.
