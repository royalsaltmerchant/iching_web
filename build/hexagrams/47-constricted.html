I Ching Hexagram 47

䷮ Constricted

Constricted / Confining / Oppression (Exhaustion)

Chinese困
Pinyinkùn
Binary010110

Reading

Reaching an impasse, there is exhaustion.

The earthly realm seeks the celestial, but heavenly energy has not passed through. Contentions will lead to extremes—do not be deterred by weariness. Do not let conduct be affected—preservere, with firmness and correctness, and there may yet be good of it. Do not exhaust the mind, and there is joy—random thoughts do not occur to Great People, so they are not influenced by circumstance. Instead, polish and refine. Gather yourself in difficulty, as you do in ease—joyful in trouble. The Way is perilous, so step firmly, and be prepared for risk—the superior person will sacrifice their life to their purpose. This is sincerity. This is blameless.

Changing Lines

Line 1: Entering the dark valley, sitting on a tree stump, unseen for years.

Weakness here is self-destruction; follow the powerful yang lines to avoid resignation. Those who give up at the slightest difficulty, will never make it out of the valley.

Line 2: Stand up straight amongst the larder.

Sustenance is lacking. There will be hardship, but the Dao provides to those who stay true. There is success after adversity. Maintain sincerity, and there will be no blame.

Line 3: Meeting with thorns, not seeing your partner, caught up on rocks.

A very inauspicious line. Acting arbitrarily, pretending to follow the Dao, only leads to exhaustion. Honour teachers, and respect companions—there's no real constriction here at all.

Line 4: Moving cautiously, there will hardship, but with a good ending.

The aim is misplaced towards strengthening weak lines, but good can be unearthed. When things are difficult, try a little more. The lines suggest you have everything needed to move through this to re-established joy. It could be slow and painful, but you have the resources.

Line 5: In leisure and satisfaction, the nose and feet are cut off.

You are well aimed in the Dao, though your spiritual and earthly purpose is stifled. Make yourself small and humble, and continue doing the right thing, and it is only a matter of time before the Dao is fulfilled, and the impasse is overcome. Enter with sincerity.

Line 6: Vines and creepers hold you fast and straight.

You know if you move, you'll find reason to repent, because you're ignorant of causes and conditions. Do not pretend—pretending to be great is to suffer without the opportunity to withdraw. Seek out help and teachers, and only act after true understanding. Repentance will break the standstill, and you will join the Great Path.
