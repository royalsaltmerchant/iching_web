I Ching Hexagram 6

䷅ Dispute

Dispute / Conflict / Arguing

Chinese訟
Pinyinsòng
Binary010111

Reading

Truth is obstructed, but be cautious and moderate, rather than seeking a final solution.

Though you are sincere in your current dispute, truth is blocked, and the path of prosecution will be met with obstruction—if pushed to a finale, the ending will be evil. Be your greater person by moderation and cautiousness—good fortune will come of it. Great sages know they can control their temperament—others will react with poison if pushed, deviating from harmony in a power struggle. To change, be rid of scheming and impulsiveness—it is better to contend with yourself than with others, though to be alive to sometimes suffer the scheming of others. If there is someone wiser who can settle it for you, seek them out. Now is not the time to act; there is no sure footing.

Changing Lines

Line 1: Do not persist, and things will turn out well with little criticism.

By not continuing the matter, being flexible and deferential, you will suffer the ill words of others in exchange for good fortune yourself.

Line 2: No pressing the issue, escape and return amongst places you are safe.

You may be unequal to this dispute, unprepared or hitting above your weight. If you retreat and conceal yourself, you will be make no further mistakes, rather than inviting calamity.

Line 3: Hold to the virtues you've established, and keep your integrity, not acting where others are affected.

Though dispute is perilous, you keep firm to the places you already know you have support. Wise friends that can clarify the issue will lead to good fortune, as others will be unable to claim merit against them.

Line 4: Not pressing the dispute, letting fate, one may rest in virtue and integrity, to good fortune.

You are unequal to this dispute, so let it rest; return to study, change your desire to be contentious, and rest where things are firm and correct—being adamant with oneself is profitable.

Line 5: Dispute well, and good will come of it.

You are holding the proper mean; balanced and correctly oriented. Pursuing your contention will be auspicious, if you hold to virtue.

Line 6: Even if you are rewarded, you will lose three times that amount if you do not move forward with respect.

Though you may win the dispute, it will not end in your respect—this is the outcome of victory without virtue, and allowing scheming and impulsiveness to win the day. Be cautious—if you will resort to being selfish and inconsiderate, it is better to not be contentious.
