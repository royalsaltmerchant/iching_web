I Ching Hexagram 54

䷵ Marrying Off the Maiden

Marrying Off the Maiden / Converting the Maiden / The Marrying Maiden

Chinese歸妹
Pinyinguī mèi
Binary110100

Reading

The young maiden is to be married, but without advantage.

The marriage is a forming of union between heaven and earth, but in seeking enjoyment, and obeying emotion, the real is sought in the false. When habits form, and incorrect action and desire are set, yin and yang go awry, and heaven and earth do not settle naturally without effort. The east is host, your essence—the west is guest, your feeling. The thunder, your previous merit, is in the east, but a lake, seeking punishment, will swallow it all, dissipating positive energy. Follow your merit. Evolve from maiden to mother. Keep in mind the far-distant end to ward off pleasure seeking that may result in bad beginnings. Essence is not found in emotion.

Changing Lines

Line 1: The maiden will be secondary in the husband's eyes, but the lame will walk.

Though there be a lameness to the connection between heaven and earth, the maiden can take the second position, providing great help. Maintain integrity, and external objects can not impose their defilement. Practice in this way, and you will walk.

Line 2: With one eye, you can still see. Chastity will prevail.

Though there may be a blindness to the connection between heaven and earth, the solitary widow can achieve constancy. Be flexible in strength, and like the one-eyed who can see, you are outwardly dark, and inwardly bright. Consider essence as primary, be chaste and single-minded in the Dao, and in seclusion you will know true pleasure. Do not pursue what is not right.

Line 3: Turning back to proper orientation, the maiden makes a great second.

Improprieties have led to the maiden being wedded off into less than ideal status, though she can be a proper secondary wife. In weakness, the orientation is off, and externals pierce your nose to draw you off like cattle. Turn from error.

Line 4: When it is the wrong time to marry, delay until conditions are right.

Though the time is late to be married, still the maiden may make a good wife. The Dao is focused stillness, yielding in strength—if the mind of Dao is set before action is required, then the outcomes can be fortuitous. Move from wrong to right, and await the proper time.

Line 5: The Emperor's younger sister marries—the timing is auspicious.

With noble character, even a scheming half-sister can not usurp the maiden's position. Settled, virtuous, and deferential to the wise and noble, those who foster the mind of Dao are able to shine. All the better if you can find wise aid. The moon is full of sunlight—here and now, good fortune is found.

Line 6: Sacrifice without blood, an empty chest—there is no benefit here.

With an empty basket, there will be no advantage to this union. Ignorantly fulfilling yin and yang through conditions, being indulgent, seeking to force immortality, is like being gifted an empty box—though you may appear fortunate, this behaviour will only lead you to failure.
