I Ching Hexagram 28

䷛ Excess of Greatness

Excess of Greatness / Great Exceeding / Preponderance of the Great

Chinese大過
Pinyindà guò
Binary011110

Reading

The house beam sags under the weight an excess of greatness—moving in any direction, there will be development.

Whatever ventured has the possibility of success, but the supporting beam of the house bends. It is best when weak lines are on the inside, with strong on the outside—hexagram 28 is the opposite. Without change, the whole structure could crumble under the pressure. The timing is extraordinary—use emptiness in fullness for the highest effect. Not knowing what is enough, and the house will fall. Indulgently pursuing the strong yang energy, seeking external desires and delights and self-satisfaction, the beam could fracture. The Sun is the center of the hexagram—see things as they are, looking to what's important to the situation, and do not force things. With an excess of strong lines, here, weak lines must provide the support. With enough ying energy, proceed with flexibility, being easygoing and adaptable, and development will be unhindered. This is gentle penetration. Stand tall, solitary, without fear, if need be, retiring from the world without blame or regret, and you will be like the Great People. All conditions are temporary.

Changing Lines

Line 1: The white rushes are set under things.

Feel your weakness, and react appropriately—like setting down a soft blanket before placing something fragile, use exceptional care and prudence to avoid error.

Line 2: The old willow grows new shoots.

Just as how it's soft water that makes old willows strong, conditions may seem unusual, but there is advantage and vitality here. In balancing strength with the ability to yield, firmness met with flexibility, all associations are extraordinary—the high and the low can work together to great success.

Line 3: The beam is weak—there will be evil.

Evil is connected to the weak foundation of the hexagram—these are the condition if one insists on charging forward heedlessly, avoiding the support of good people. Know when to withdraw before the house beam snaps.

Line 4: The beam is braced, bending back upwards to better fortune.

Finding good help is advantageous, but if you seek personal gain by exploiting the goodwill of others, you will be humiliated. The strong lines are firm, so follow with yielding—too small, too flexible, and the strength of the house will deteriorate.

Line 5: The decayed willow flowers.

When tired, there is no need for concern, or regret, though it is unlikely there will be advantage. When the tree is old, it's flowering may only hasten it's end; luxury and self-satisfaction injures the real with the false—when the belly is filled, empty the mind, and rest without blame. Apply inner strength with balance, and do not disparage those who have aided you.

Line 6: Wading the stream with incredible boldness.

Attempting to ford the current, until the waters cover the crown of your head, this is extraordinary energy. This will end in evil, but without blame for the effort. Act arbitrarily, in ignorance, and the destruction could be great. Go too far, and lose good fortune. Return to prudence if possible, but, by your own heart, there may be things more important than your own well-being.
